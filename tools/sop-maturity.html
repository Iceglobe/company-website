S&OP Maturity Self-Assessment

Six minutes. 12 questions. A personal benchmark on the 5-level maturity scale we use to advise supply chain leaders. Built by ops practitioners out of Novo Nordisk, Ambu, and WSA.

How the S&OP maturity assessment works

Twelve questions cover the parts of the planning cycle that decide whether S&OP produces decisions or just slides: demand consensus, forecast accuracy, master data, safety stock, capacity, scenarios, meeting output and how well knowledge survives when a planner moves on. Each answer scores 0-4 points, for a total between 0 and 48. Your total places you on one of five levels.

The five levels

  • Level 1, Reactive (0-11): planning runs on spreadsheets and quick reactions. The team keeps things moving through sheer effort.
  • Level 2, Structured (12-21): there is a monthly cadence and the key numbers are measured, but the S&OP meeting is mostly a status update.
  • Level 3, Integrated (22-30): planning is a real discipline and the meeting produces decisions. The tooling is often what limits the next step.
  • Level 4, Optimized (31-38): the foundations are solid. The gains now come from scenario planning, automation and sharper safety stock.
  • Level 5, Autonomous (39-48): routine planning work runs largely by itself, and planners spend their time on judgement calls and strategy.

How to use your result

The level matters less than the questions where you scored lowest. Those are the specific habits holding the cycle back, and most companies move up a level by fixing two or three of them rather than by replacing everything at once. Run the assessment again after a quarter to see whether the changes held.

Answer for how planning actually runs on a normal month, not how the process document says it should. Taking it with your demand and supply planners separately is a good way to find where the views differ.

Where the model comes from

The five levels follow the same staged logic as widely used S&OP maturity models, such as Gartner's, adapted to mid-market manufacturers and the questions we ask when we review a planning process with a new customer. The scoring is simple on purpose: it is a starting point for a conversation, not an audit. If you want to talk through your result, book a 20-minute call.
