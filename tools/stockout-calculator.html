How the stockout calculator works

A stockout rarely costs only the order you couldn't ship. The calculator adds up four effects, all from the numbers you enter above, so you can see where the money actually goes.

  • Lost revenue: the share of demand you couldn't supply and never recovered. Revenue × stockout rate × (1 − recovery rate).
  • Lost gross margin: what that lost revenue would have earned you. This is the direct profit hit.
  • Expedite cost: the orders you did save, usually through rush freight, overtime or a re-planned production run. It assumes an average order of $8,000 and your expedite cost per order.
  • Customer-lifetime damage: some customers who hit a stockout move part of their future orders elsewhere. The multiplier says how many orders' worth of margin each lost order puts at risk.

Every line is counted at gross margin, not revenue, so the total reads as a profit impact you can put next to an investment case.

Where to find your inputs

  • Stockout rate: order lines not shipped on the requested date, from your ERP's order-fill or OTIF report. If you only track it for top customers, start there.
  • Recovery rate: how many of those late lines were shipped later rather than cancelled.
  • Expedite cost: the extra freight and overtime on rescued orders, often visible in freight invoices tagged express.
  • Lifetime multiplier: start low (2-3) unless you have churn data that says otherwise. Sales usually knows which accounts went quiet after a missed delivery.

How to read the result

Treat the total as a range rather than a precise figure. Change one input at a time to see which one drives most of the cost: for most manufacturers it is the stockout rate itself, which is exactly the number better planning moves. A fill-rate improvement of a few percentage points often pays for the planning work on its own.
