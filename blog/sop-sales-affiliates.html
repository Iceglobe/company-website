Which Sales Affiliates Belong in Your S&OP Process?

Every company runs its sales network differently. For S&OP you only need to answer one question: who accounts for most of your sales, and most of your demand variability?

TL;DR

Include the few markets that drive most of your volume and most of your surprises, usually a handful of names. If you have more than six or seven affiliates in the review, you have probably gone too far. Stop adding once an extra affiliate no longer improves delivery performance, forecast accuracy or inventory.

Watch the 3-minute video this article is based on, or see the full S&OP training series.

Sales channels come in many shapes

Depending on size, set-up and industry, a manufacturer may sell through:

  • Sales affiliates: subsidiaries owned by the parent company that serve a region or country.
  • Distributors: resellers that sell your product with a markup, think of a grocery store.
  • Direct sales: the parent company sells straight to the customer.

The list goes on. Companies are imaginative and try many channels to see what creates the most value.

The one question that decides who to include

For S&OP, only one thing matters: who accounts for most of your sales, and who accounts for most of the variability in your demand? That list is usually short. Hopefully a handful of names, and those are the ones to include consistently every month.

With an affiliate set-up, that is typically your largest markets, for example Germany, France, the UK and the US. The affiliates that create the most variability differ from company to company and are often tender-driven. If you work in sales or operations, you already know them: they are the ones that kick the door in once or twice a year with a huge order and put everyone into firefighting mode.

How many is too many?

Be selective. If more than six or seven affiliates sit in your S&OP review, you have probably gone a little too far down the rabbit hole.

The goal isn't to capture everything. Every affiliate you add means more time collecting and analysing data and less time on decisions. It costs the affiliates too: every hour they spend on forecasting is an hour not spent selling.

How to know you've found the sweet spot

Aim to capture just enough. You have hit the sweet spot when adding one more affiliate no longer meaningfully improves your metrics: delivery performance, forecast accuracy and inventory buffers.

Once you know who to talk to, the next step is coaching them on what input you need, and how to handle tenders, launches and new customers in the forecast.
