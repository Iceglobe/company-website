Supply chain planning software for mid-market manufacturers: Kinaxis vs o9 vs Anaplan vs Quiver vs OmniaOps in 2026

Yes, I built one of the five products in this comparison, so consider the source. The take below is based on what each vendor publishes about itself, read by someone who has spent years running supply chain planning, and it is the one I would write whether or not OmniaOps existed.

TL;DR

Kinaxis is best for very large complex networks where supply-side multi-echelon optimization is the binding constraint. o9 is best where the buyer needs a configurable enterprise platform with deep customization budget and timeline. Anaplan is best where planning is more about modeling than about operational execution. Quiver is best for small e-commerce and consumer brands (under roughly 50 people) that mainly need demand forecasting and purchase suggestions on top of Shopify, Amazon or Business Central. OmniaOps is best for mid-market manufacturers ($50M-$750M) that need fast deployment, customization without consulting overhead, and a platform that runs the planning process with them rather than another tool the team has to work around.

The honest disclaimer

I am the founder of OmniaOps. Before building OmniaOps, I led supply chain planning and operations teams at Novo Nordisk, Ambu, and WSA. I have not run S&OP on Kinaxis, o9, Anaplan or Quiver myself, so the vendor sections below are built on each vendor's public documentation and announcements as of 2026, read through that planning experience. Treat them as an informed outside view and test the claims in a demo. I have tried to be precise about strengths and weaknesses, not generous to any product, including ours.

Kinaxis

Kinaxis (RapidResponse / Maestro)

Founded 1984. Public (TSX). Headquarters Ottawa.

Strengths: Best-in-class concurrent planning engine. The way Kinaxis re-plans the entire network when a single data point changes is genuinely industry-leading. For very large, very complex networks (think semiconductor, automotive OEM, pharma majors), nothing matches it. Strong S&OP workflow. Long track record, strong partner ecosystem. Maestro Agents (launched October 2025) and the no-code Agent Studio (February 2026) bring AI agents into the planning workflow.

Weaknesses: Implementation time and cost are the binding constraint for most mid-market buyers. The platform requires significant internal investment to maintain, including Kinaxis-specialist roles. Customization is vendor-driven and changes are slow. The UX is widely described as dated compared to newer platforms.

Buy if: You are $1B+ with a complex multi-echelon network and the budget for a 12-24 month implementation plus dedicated internal Kinaxis expertise.

Skip if: You are mid-market and the implementation timeline is longer than your patience or budget.

o9 Solutions

o9 Solutions (Digital Brain platform)

Founded 2009. Private. Headquarters Dallas.

Strengths: Highly configurable. Strong AI/ML capabilities, with AI agents built into the Digital Brain since 2024-2025 and the APEX planning model announced in March 2026. Broad functional coverage from demand sensing through integrated business planning. Good for buyers who want a "platform" rather than a tool.

Weaknesses: Implementation requires a partner. Time-to-value is long. Configuration depth is double-edged: it can do almost anything, which means it usually takes a year+ to do the specific thing you actually need. Pricing is high.

Buy if: You are $500M+ with the budget for partner-led implementation and a strategic decision to build a "digital twin" of your supply chain.

Skip if: You need to be in production within 6 months or you do not have a dedicated planning-tech team.

Anaplan

Anaplan

Founded 2006. Private (Thoma Bravo since 2022). Headquarters Miami.

Strengths: Connected planning across functions (FP&A, sales, supply chain). Modeler-driven configuration is empowering for analytical teams. Strong scenario planning. Easier to bring in-house than Kinaxis or o9 once the initial model is built. Role-based AI agents (December 2025) and Agent Studio (March 2026) are adding AI assistance on top.

Weaknesses: Anaplan is a planning calculation engine, not a supply chain execution platform. You can build S&OP and demand planning in it, but the operational ergonomics (exception management, alerts, role-based workflows) are not native. Performance can degrade on very large models. Anaplan-specialist talent is a real constraint.

Buy if: Your planning challenge is primarily analytical/financial rather than operational, and you want a flexible platform that your team can own.

Skip if: You need turnkey operational supply chain workflows out of the box.

Quiver

Quiver

Founded 2023. Private. Headquarters Copenhagen.

Strengths: Focused and easy to grasp: demand forecasting, daily inventory calculations and automatic purchase order suggestions, aimed at replacing the Excel planning sheet. Ready-made integrations to the systems small brands actually run on, including Shopify, Amazon, Business Central, NAV and Uniconta. A shared view of sales, inventory and finance. Danish team close to Nordic e-commerce brands.

Weaknesses: Quiver shows S&OP-style views, but it gives you a forecast rather than facilitating the process around it. There is no place for sales or planners to override the forecast by hand, comment on why, or run scenarios, so the discussion and the decisions still happen in email, spreadsheets and meetings outside the tool. Pricing and implementation time are not published.

Buy if: You are a small e-commerce or consumer brand (under roughly 50 people) that mainly needs a good statistical forecast and purchase suggestions.

Skip if: You need several people to work the forecast together, with overrides, comments and scenarios, and a monthly S&OP process that brings sales, supply and finance to one decision.

OmniaOps

OmniaOps

Founded 2026. Private. Based in Greater Copenhagen. Small team, founder-led.

Strengths: Built for mid-market manufacturers specifically. End-to-end planning (demand, supply, inventory, capacity) in one platform. More than a tool: the platform carries the planning process it serves, and this is what we do best. Sales, supply chain and finance work in the same plan: people override the forecast where they know better, comment on why, and run scenarios side by side, and each step of the S&OP cycle (inputs, review, decision, follow-up) is built into the workflow. Several people work on it together and the process runs the way it is meant to, instead of around spreadsheets and slide decks. Weeks-not-years deployment: working on your own data in under two weeks, full ERP integration in 4-6 weeks. Co-build customization model: we adapt the platform to your process, not the other way around. Founder team are practitioners. AI agents that carry out routine planner tasks come later: we introduce them 6-12 months in, once the process and data are mature enough for agents to work reliably in day-to-day operations.

Weaknesses: We are early. Limited partner ecosystem (still building it). Smaller team means less coverage if you need 24/7 multi-region support today. Less proven on the very largest deployments (>$1B revenue). Brand awareness lower than the incumbents.

Buy if: You are mid-market, you need fast deployment, your planning runs in Excel today and you need a platform that respects how your team actually works.

Skip if: You are a Fortune 500 company that needs a vendor with 20 years of references and 10,000 implementations behind them.

The questions that actually matter when choosing

Forget the feature matrix. Kinaxis, o9, Anaplan and OmniaOps all cover demand planning, MRP, S&OP and inventory optimization (Quiver focuses on the forecast and purchasing). Among the four that do, the features are not the differentiator. These are:

  1. Implementation time and cost. Multiply the vendor's stated number by 1.5 for an honest estimate. Ask for three references at your size and ask them what the real number was.
  2. Customization model. When your process changes (and it will), can you change the platform in a week, or does it require a 6-month consulting engagement?
  3. Where does the daily operational work happen? The vendor demo will show you the polished views. Ask to see what a planner does every day. Ask to see exception handling, not the dashboards.
  4. What is the upgrade path? Some platforms make it easy to add modules incrementally. Others make every new module a fresh implementation.
  5. What happens when the implementation partner leaves? Some platforms are functional without the partner. Some are not. Find out which kind you are buying.

A pragmatic shortlist heuristic

  • Revenue > $2B, complex multi-echelon, budget > $2M for software + impl: shortlist Kinaxis + o9.
  • Revenue $500M-$2B, you want a strategic platform: shortlist o9 + Anaplan.
  • Revenue $200M-$500M, planning runs in Excel today: shortlist OmniaOps + Anaplan + Netstock.
  • Revenue $50M-$200M, you need to be live this year: shortlist OmniaOps + Netstock + Inventoro.
  • Under 50 people, e-commerce or consumer brand that mainly needs a forecast and purchase suggestions: shortlist Quiver.

Closing

The supply chain planning market is not "winner take all". Different vendors fit different problems. The most expensive mistake I have seen mid-market manufacturers make is buying a vendor that fits a Fortune 500 problem and then spending three years failing to deploy it. Choose the vendor whose target customer profile matches yours, not the one with the biggest analyst-report rating.
